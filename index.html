Before work begins

List every planned upgrade before work is priced.

Is your home recently built, with builder rough-ins in the walls? These and the panel labels may not match what was finished, so compare the labels and any builder drawings with the equipment you plan to add. Older homes can have outdated labels too.

If the home is still under a builder warranty, read what the documents say about who may work on the installation. Then write one combined list of the equipment you plan to add, so the contractor can assess the supply for all of it.

Can the existing panel carry a heat pump, range and charger together?

Not necessarily, and the answer depends on the whole installation rather than on spare breaker spaces. Ask the contractor to assess the combined demand of everything planned and to explain the result in plain terms before equipment is bought.

Which upgrade should be installed first when several are planned?

The order can matter, because an early installation may use up supply that a later one needs. A contractor can propose a sequence and state what each stage prepares for the next.

Why do two proposals for the same upgrades differ so much in scope?

Each quote may assume something different about the existing supply and include different items. Set them side by side against one written list of the planned equipment, and note what each includes, leaves out and assumes.

Who should I ask about builder paperwork on a newly finished home?

Start with the seller or the builder for drawings, inspection records and warranty papers. Read the warranty documents yourself, because they may say who should work on the installation. Share copies with the contractor.

Once my enquiry is reviewed, who signs for the electrical work?

A hired provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor does; this site offers planning guidance. Your enquiry is first taken in and reviewed, but the site neither organises visits nor quotes prices, and neither signs for nor does electrical work. That provider confirms its name and licence separately, then agrees the job and its price and dates with you.
