Panel and service upgrades in Waterdown.

A sub-panel for a Waterdown garage, basement or addition adds circuit positions at a distance, but it draws from the same supply as everything else. That makes the combined list of planned equipment the first thing to settle.

A panel and an electrical service are different parts of the system

The panel distributes electricity to property circuits. The service is the incoming supply and associated equipment. Replacing a panel does not automatically increase the available supply. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess existing condition and demand, planned loads and the utility requirements before recommending equipment. Spare breaker spaces alone do not establish capacity.

Prepare the scope for your Waterdown property

Take photographs of the panel's labels and door from outside, and note any provision the builder may have left, such as a capped conduit. List each space the sub-panel would serve and what will run there.

Ask whether the main panel and supply can carry the sub-panel together with the other planned upgrades. Ask also how the feeder route will be chosen, and whether the existing labels will be corrected to match the finished work.

What should the assessment establish?

How much the main panel and supply can carry, once the sub-panel's area and every other planned upgrade are counted together, is what the contractor calculates. The feeder route comes next: the contractor traces where it will run and names whoever must open walls or ceilings to route it. The contractor also explains how the new circuits are labelled and how the main panel directory is updated. Each load the Waterdown sub-panel will serve is listed in the scope with its intended location. On the ESA side, the scope also names the notification of work.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can a basement sub-panel be added before the rest of the finishing?

It can be easier to route a feeder before walls and ceilings are closed, but the plan should still account for later loads. Ask the contractor which circuits to provide now and which positions to leave empty for later.

What if the builder's panel labels do not match the finished rooms?

Labels written during construction may not match the finished rooms. Record what each label says and note any room it seems to miss, then ask the contractor to review and correct the directory as part of the work.

Useful project details

  • Equipment location and access restrictions
