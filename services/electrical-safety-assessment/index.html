A useful assessment has a stated purpose and boundary

A condition concern, renovation decision or insurer request can each call for a different assessment. Agree which equipment and areas will be reviewed, whether testing is included and what written result will be provided. Concealed wiring and inaccessible equipment may limit conclusions. A general visit should not be represented as complete certification of everything in the property.

Prepare the scope for your Waterdown property

Collect earlier inspection reports, ESA paperwork and builder drawings, then write down each major load you plan to add. Note where the panel and meter are and who can give the contractor access on the day.

Ask which equipment and rooms the visit will cover, whether testing is included and what the report will state. Ask which parts the contractor expects to be unable to judge because they are hidden or blocked.

What should the assessment establish?

Where a report lists findings but never says why the review was done, a Waterdown homeowner adding a major load cannot tell what it covered. The report names its purpose, since a condition concern, a renovation decision and an insurer request call for different reviews. Items seen directly stay apart from those behind finishes or out of reach. Findings appear in ordinary words, each with any further assessment needed, and the next step's scope says if an ESA notification applies. The contractor relates the planned equipment to the existing panel and supply but should not call one visit an approval of everything.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can an insurer or lender ask for a written electrical review?

Some insurers or lenders may ask for a written report before they proceed. Ask them what the report must cover, then tell the contractor so that the agreed scope matches what the third party needs.

Does a review before new equipment cover the whole house?

No. A review has a stated boundary, and concealed or inaccessible parts may limit what can be concluded. Request a written note from the contractor of what was included, what was left out and why.
